# Consultus Human-Led Writing Rules

Jake's mandatory copy standard for everything written in this project: pages, case studies, articles, decks, proposals. Supplied July 2026 after weak La Vie article drafts. The lesson: the strongest writing was not the most elaborate. It had clear judgment, concrete examples, fewer words, and no pressure to sound sophisticated.

## Core instruction

Write like an experienced human strategist or subject-matter expert, not an AI content generator. Direct, specific, useful, credible. Do not try to make the writing sound sophisticated. Make it clear.

## The rules

1. **Concrete over abstract.** The reader must be able to picture what is happening. Banned: metabolic ecosystem, cognitive vitality, sustainable transformation, optimize outcomes, elevate performance, holistic journey, unlock potential, comprehensive solution, seamless experience, dynamic landscape, tailored approach, meaningful weight reduction, long-term wellness goals. Prefer: what is affecting your weight, why the scale is not moving, longer appointments, one team working from the same medical file.

2. **Do not inflate simple ideas.** Say it once, plainly. "The goal is to help you lose fat, preserve muscle, and improve your long-term metabolic health" beats a paragraph about meaningful improvements in vitality and healthspan.

3. **No default AI sentence rhythms.** These may appear occasionally, never as the pattern: "It is not simply X. It is Y." / "The goal is not just X, but Y" / "Whether you are X or Y" / "From X to Y" / "In today's fast-paced world" / "At its core" / "The result?" / "This is where X comes in" / "It is important to note" / "When it comes to" / "Ultimately" / "By combining X with Y" / "This approach ensures".

4. **Write from observed situations, not personas.** "Waiting months for a family doctor is a problem when your child keeps getting ear infections" beats "Families seeking accessible pediatric care may benefit from private clinic services."

5. **Use judgment.** State what matters, what does not, who the service is for, who it is not for, what to watch out for, where the limitations are. "Are you getting what you want from the care you have now? If you are, stay where you are." Balanced advice creates more trust than constant selling.

6. **Never repeat the heading in the opening sentence.** The first sentence under a heading must advance the article.

7. **Lead with the useful information.** Put the answer before the explanation. "A comprehensive health assessment generally costs between $2,500 and $5,000" first, context after.

8. **Cut filler transitions.** Delete: It is worth noting that, It is important to understand that, With that being said, That said, In many cases, As mentioned earlier, As a result, In addition, Furthermore, Moreover, In other words, Ultimately, At the end of the day.

9. **Minimal qualifiers.** Use generally / typically / often / may / can / tends to only when accuracy requires them, not to soften every statement.

10. **No empty claims.** Never call a service innovative, cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class, industry-leading, premium, comprehensive, personalized, results-driven, or advanced unless the next sentence proves it. Show the evidence instead of naming the quality.

11. **"Personalized" earns its place.** Explain what actually changes per person: "The physician adjusts the plan based on your bloodwork, body composition, medical history, medication response, and goals."

12. **Short paragraphs.** One to three sentences. Editorial, not templated. No walls of text, and not every sentence in its own paragraph.

13. **Lists must earn their place.** Only when the reader is comparing services, criteria, steps, symptoms, inclusions, questions, or costs. Items parallel and specific ("Visceral fat", not "Enhanced performance").

14. **Do not summarize every list.** Never intro + list + restatement of the same list. Keep two of the three at most.

15. **No forced sophistication.** "Disciplined effort is not producing results" beats "metabolic resistance to weight loss." Medical terms clarify the service, never decorate the writing.

16. **Informed, not academic.** Use a technical term when the patient will see it in a report, it matters to understanding the service, or people search for it. Define it on first use.

17. **Credible marketing, not promotional copy.** Roughly 70% useful information, 20% service explanation, 10% conversion language. The page should still help the reader if the company name were removed. Do not mention the company in every section.

18. **Specific CTAs.** "Speak with a Patient Advisor about which assessment fits your needs" or "Book a consultation at the Oakville clinic." Never "Learn more", "Get started today", "Begin your journey".

19. **No fake empathy.** Cut "We understand how frustrating this journey can be" unless followed by something specific and useful ("a physician can test for insulin resistance, thyroid issues, or hormonal changes").

20. **Preserve natural imperfection.** Simple declarative sentences, deliberate fragments, uneven lengths, clear opinions, practical caveats. Not every paragraph perfectly balanced.

## SEO rules

21. **Search intent before keywords.** Identify what the reader is asking, worried about, and deciding. Answer in that order. Do not seed the keyword into every section.

22. **Keywords naturally.** Primary phrase in title, opening, one or two headings, meta title/description, and the close. Rotate natural variations (private health clinic / private clinic / physician-led clinic) instead of repeating one phrase.

23. **No word-count padding.** Stop when the topic is answered. A tight 1,500 words beats a padded 2,500. No FAQs that repeat the body.

24. **Headings sound like real questions or decisions.** "What does a private clinic cost?" / "Do you need a referral?" Never "Exploring the Benefits of Private Care" or "Your Path to Improved Wellness".

25. **Original value.** Every article needs at least three elements competitors lack: a clear judgment, a useful comparison, a concrete example, a practical warning, a realistic limitation, a decision framework, company-specific operational detail, or first-hand expertise.

## Medical and regulated content

26. **No overstated outcomes.** No guarantees, universal claims, implied diagnoses, or absolute safety claims. Use: may include, when clinically appropriate, based on the physician's assessment, results vary by person, some services may be covered depending on the plan.

27. **Separate facts from positioning.** A fact is verifiable ("The assessment includes DEXA body composition testing"). Positioning must not pretend to be fact.

28. **Name the expert.** Reviewer name, credentials, role, clinic, review date. Not a generic "medically reviewed".

## Formatting

29. Sentence case for headings. Bold only for headings, labels, deliberate emphasis. Minimal italics and colons. No em dashes. No exclamation marks. Concise bullets. Descriptive link text, never "click here".

## Final editing pass

Before submitting, delete or rewrite any sentence that:
1. Adds no new information
2. Could be said in fewer words
3. Doesn't sound like a real person
4. Isn't specific enough to be useful
5. Makes a claim without evidence
6. Repeats the heading or previous paragraph
7. Uses an abstract phrase where a concrete example is clearer
8. Contains an AI cliche or predictable structure
9. Mentions the company unnecessarily
10. Would leave the reader no worse off if removed

## Multi-pass workflow for long-form

1. **Outline:** search intent, reader questions, required facts, decision criteria, objections, brand-specific evidence, medical/legal caveats.
2. **Draft plainly:** simple language, no attempt to sound premium.
3. **Humanize:** cut generic transitions, abstract language, repeated explanations, AI patterns. Replace broad claims with concrete examples. Make the clearest defensible judgment.
4. **Compress 20%:** delete repetition, filler, delayed answers, restated summaries. Keep examples, evidence, caveats, decision info.
5. **AI-pattern audit:** flag and rewrite anything that could appear on any competitor's website.
