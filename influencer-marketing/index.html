Influencer marketing, explained

What is influencer marketing?

Influencer marketing is a kind of social media marketing, and it’s all about building trust. Your customers expect you to highlight everything that makes your brand the best. To validate it, they need to hear it from fellow consumers: real people sharing real stories, trusted by the audiences who follow them.

Unlike traditional channels that rely on celebrity star power, influencers are everyday consumers who have mastered engagement across Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, LinkedIn, and YouTube. Whether it’s product experiences or industry insight, they wield effective storytelling and have earned a reputation for their voice, aesthetic, and expertise. They can be anyone in any niche with a real following, from a few thousand to millions.

User-generated content refers to images, videos, reviews, testimonials, or posts created and shared by creators in collaboration with a brand. Its significance lies in authenticity: it carries higher credibility among other consumers because it reads as unbiased and genuine. A UGC creator is a regular consumer who actively collaborates with brands to produce that content, and they play a real part in shaping how a brand is perceived.

Why it matters

Why is influencer marketing important?

Consumers are constantly inundated by ads. Now they want to hear from real people with real stories they can trust. Influencers have built that reputation for authenticity, and the market has followed them to it.

The return is unusually high

Done well, influencer marketing returns far more than it costs and outperforms traditional banner advertising by a wide margin. That is why so many brands selling online already work with influencers.

The power of authenticity

Consumers are inclined to trust honest product reviews from vetted influencers who have built a loyal community. That community is a pool of potentially loyal customers who arrive already predisposed to believe the recommendation.

From engaged followers to engaged customers

With a sizable audience, an influencer can amplify your brand, drive followers to your own channels, and build a pipeline that runs from engaged follower through to happy customer rather than stopping at awareness.

Increased conversions

Not getting enough clicks on ads? Influencer marketing takes the guesswork out of customer profiling. Influencers serve up a receptive audience already prepared to buy, so each recommendation converts at a rate cold targeting rarely matches.

Much of the influencer marketing budget goes to Instagram, where highly visual content performs and creators can lean on interactive features. But the discipline has spread well beyond it, and LinkedIn creators including recruiters and founders are now a serious channel for B2B.

Who needs it?

Who needs influencer marketing?

Consumers trust brands they know, and more so when those brands are endorsed by people they know. Influencer marketing takes that trust to a larger audience, curated by the most capable storytellers. It’s word-of-mouth at its best, rewarding brands with visibility, social proof, and the ability to turn new followers into future brand ambassadors.

Influencers work best when they collaborate with brands on highly visual content created through their own authentic experience: candid reviews, how-to tips, product guides, recommendations, and before-and-after visuals. These are the industries where that lands hardest:

01 / Lifestyle

Travel, food, and hospitality

Hotels, resorts, restaurants, cafes, and food brands benefit from creators sharing experiences, photos, and video. It inspires potential visitors, builds anticipation, and influences decisions that are emotional as much as practical.

02 / Personal

Fashion, beauty, and fitness

Highly effective territory, where customers already love sharing outfit ideas, makeup looks, and product reviews. UGC shows real people using and styling products, which drives engagement and shapes purchase decisions.

03 / Commerce

E-commerce and consumer tech

Online retailers use creators to show real people using their products, which provides social proof, lifts conversions, reduces returns, and builds a community around the brand rather than just a customer list.

Not on the list? Influencers are everywhere and emerging across every industry. Entertainment, media, technology, and professional services all now have credible creators with engaged followings, which means the question is rarely whether the voices exist and usually whether you can find and vet them.

Practical decisions

How much does influencer marketing cost?

In a reputation-based shopping journey, influencers and UGC creators do invaluable work shaping trust and loyalty. But from product giveaways through to cost-effective rates per post, the range is wide. Seven things set the price.

Scope / 01

Campaign

What are you promoting? Your goals largely determine the spend, from the number of influencers required through to the types of content needed.

Audience / 02

Influencer reach

The size of a creator’s audience. Nano-influencers have up to 10k followers, micro up to 50k, mid-tier up to 500k, macro up to 1M, and mega above that.

Channels / 03

Platforms

Is the collaboration limited to Instagram or spanning several channels? Creators tailor content per platform and audience, which is why rates vary between them.

Assets / 04

Content

Rates vary with the number and type of posts, how complex creation is for their niche, whose channels it is posted on, and how long it takes to produce.

Depth / 05

Engagement

More than vanity metrics like follower count, it is worth investing in creators who deliver active audience engagement through genuine community management.

Timing / 06

Seasonal demand

Depending on their niche, certain creators are in far higher demand during peak shopping seasons. Lock collaborations in early and budget accordingly.

Terms / 07

Usage rights, exclusivity, and agency fee

Want to reuse the content in your own ads, or beat competitors to partnering with the most influential creators in your category? Outline usage and partnership terms early, because retrofitting them later is expensive. The agency fee covers the part most brands find hardest: finding the right influencers and UGC creators in the first place, vetting them properly, and managing the relationships. For brands running campaigns in-house, finding the right creators is usually the biggest challenge.

What can you expect from an influencer campaign?

From sponsored content to giveaways and hashtag campaigns, fueled by user-generated content, the possibilities for brand awareness are wide. Success comes down to relationship-building: a genuine collaboration promoting products creators like and their followers can grow to like too.

01 / Goals

Set your campaign and audience

Identify what you want to promote and how to reach your target audience. Focus the campaign on a creator’s reach, the relevance of their niche, and their genuine resonance with the people following them.

02 / Budget

Set your budget and cast

How big is the campaign? Based on that and your brand, choose between nano, micro, mid-tier, macro, and mega creators. Then screen them, ideally through an agency with an established network whose content is already vetted for quality and engagement.

03 / Craft

Develop messaging, then track and grow

Success lies in the strength of the collaboration, shaping content across formats from sponsored posts and cross-platform campaigns to live streams, contests, giveaways, and hashtag campaigns. Then measure engagement against campaign metrics and revenue, and optimize from there.

Judging the work

How can you tell good influencer marketing from bad?

It comes down to the metrics that matter. Ultimately you want a partnership with the right creator: a genuine brand advocate sharing authentic experiences with an audience that converts.

The metrics worth watching

  • Engagement metrics. Track interactions including likes, shares, comments, mentions, DMs, clicks, and follows. For every like a sponsored post earns, how many convert to customers?
  • Reach and follower count. Track views and impressions on the creator’s channels, and measure follower growth and engagement on your own.
  • Brand awareness. The best-performing content amplifies your brand and channels, with long-term effects like sustained engagement, recall, and conversions.
  • Conversions. Set a goal, whether that is follower counts, contest entries, landing page views, or revenue.
  • ROI. The ultimate measure. Calculate the return on each campaign and see how the most engaging creators and best-performing content drive revenue and loyalty.

Is your influencer marketing agency doing a good job?

  1. Creator collaborations. Your agency should be all hands on deck: vetting influencers and UGC creators, running content quality checks, and tracking engagement. They are your primary contact for managing the relationships end to end.
  2. Engagement tracking. A good agency tells a data-driven story using tracked links and referrals, impressions, content interactions, coupon code use, and follower growth.
  3. Long-term KPIs. The ultimate success is return. Your agency should be able to demonstrate a real pipeline from audience engagement through to conversions and customer acquisition, and recommend what to do next based on it.
