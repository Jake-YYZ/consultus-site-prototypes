Local SEO, explained

How does Google Business Profile optimization work?

It isn’t enough to rank on Google Search. Your brand also needs to be the first thing customers see on Google Maps. Your Google Business Profile is a listing that puts you on the map, and it’s a key component of local SEO.

Formerly known as Google My Business, it’s the digital version of a directory listing: a one-stop snapshot of your hours, address, contact details, photos, reviews, answers to frequently asked questions, and quick updates. Unlike a traditional listing, it’s tied into Google Search itself. When customers search for what they need, the most complete, fully optimized profile shows up.

When users search for products or providers in their area, Google weighs three ranking factors:

  • Relevance. How well your profile matches a customer’s search.
  • Distance. How close your location is to the customer.
  • Prominence. How reputable your business is, based on your rating, review count, links, and how well the profile is optimized.

Your local strategy succeeds when you score high on all three and beat the competition. That’s why optimization is ongoing rather than a one-time setup: complete the profile, verify every location if you have several, add images of your products and space, respond to reviews and questions, and make sure the profile includes the keywords you want to rank for.

Customers read reviews before they choose a business, and they trust the ones with reviews as proof of good service. That puts a complete profile at the core of your local strategy. It’s how you get found, on Google’s own terms of distance, relevance, and prominence.

Practical decisions

How much does profile optimization cost?

Typical Toronto market rates for a one-time local SEO analysis, research, and optimization pass, including your Google Business Profile, range from $400 to $1,700, with the average around $900. That’s a one-time engagement, though. For best results you want ongoing optimization, which typically runs from $100 to $1,000 per month.

Every strategy is unique, yours included. These are the factors that determine what it costs:

01 / Competition

Your industry and market

How competitive your industry and target markets are determines the extent of the local strategy needed to outrank the competition. A crowded category in a dense area takes more work than a specialist service in a quiet one.

02 / Footprint

How many locations

Each location needs to be targeted and verified separately to drive local rankings in its own area, so a multi-site business carries proportionally more work than a single storefront.

03 / Catalog

Products and services

The number of products and services you offer determines how broad or focused your local keyword strategy needs to be, which in turn shapes the content and ongoing optimization work.
