Meta Ads, explained

What is Meta advertising? How does it work?

Meta advertising is the paid marketing platform that runs across Facebook and Instagram. It consists of paid visual ads placed in users’ feeds, stories, reels, and the Explore tab, on both desktop and mobile.

Unlike Google Ads, which meets people who are already searching, Meta ads reach people before they search. Highly visual and fully immersive, they let brands promote strategic content to precisely defined audiences: age, gender, location, interests, life events, browsing behavior, and more. Formats range from single images and carousels to auto-play video and clickable shopping posts.

Following Facebook’s purchase of Instagram in 2012, the two platforms were fully integrated into one advertising dashboard. That means you can set up a single campaign that displays across both, eliminating the need to build and manage two, and giving the algorithm more signal to learn from.

Meta advertising lets businesses tap into the data the company holds on its users to create highly targeted ads with high conversion potential. The question is which combination of format, placement, and campaign goal your brand actually needs.

Explore the possibilities

Meta ad formats.

Meta supports different types of content across Feed, Stories, Reels, Explore, and Shopping placements, on both Facebook and Instagram.

01 / Feed

In-feed

The most organic ad format. These appear in users’ feeds on both Facebook and Instagram, looking like regular posts but carrying a Sponsored tag. Best for offers, product launches, and anything that benefits from sitting inside normal browsing.

02 / Full-screen

Stories

One of Instagram’s most popular features, Stories attract attention because they are fast, engaging, and never-ending. With each swipe, customers see quick updates, tap on links, and take you up on a call to action, so you can keep them moving toward a conversion.

03 / Browsing

Explore and discovery

The Explore tab is where users find recommended content based on the accounts they follow, their interests, and their interactions. Explore ads put your brand in front of customers in your target demographic at the exact moment they are open to finding something new.

04 / Short-form

Video and Reels

Short-form video is one of the highest-engagement formats on the platform. Reels and in-feed video give you screen time to demonstrate a product, not just claim what it does.

05 / Commerce

Shopping, carousels, and catalog ads

Carousels, collections, and Advantage+ catalog ads (formerly dynamic ads) turn a scroll into a storefront. Shopping ads let customers discover a product, see the key details, and buy in one screen, which makes them the workhorse format for e-commerce brands.

Ad types, targeting, and campaign goals.

The Meta ads platform is intuitive and highly customizable. The hard part is not launching a campaign. It is choosing the combination of ad type, audience, and objective that fits what you actually sell.

Ad types you can run

Each ad type offers different benefits: more interactivity, the ability to display multiple products, or a different user action. A good Meta campaign usually leverages several to achieve the best outcome.

  • Single image ads
  • Video ads, including Reels and Stories placements
  • Carousel ads
  • Collection ads with an Instant Experience
  • Advantage+ catalog ads (formerly dynamic ads)
  • Lead ads with instant forms
  • Messaging ads that open a chat in Messenger, Instagram, or WhatsApp
  • Boosted posts and partnership ads with creators

How Meta targets your audience

Meta advertising uses the platform’s data on its users to allow extremely precise targeting across its networks. Targeting options include:

  • Location, down to a radius around your storefront
  • Age, gender, and language
  • Education level and institution
  • Interests and life events
  • On-platform and browsing behavior
  • Connections to your page, app, or events
  • Custom audiences built from your own customer list, site traffic, or engagement
  • Lookalike audiences modeled on your best existing buyers

Campaign goals

Every Meta campaign starts with one of six objectives, and the objective tells the algorithm what to optimize for:

  • Awareness. Put your brand in front of new audiences, tap into new customer segments, and give them an attractive reason to remember you.
  • Traffic. Drive shoppers to a landing page on your site, demonstrate product features, and help customers weigh your brand during the buyer’s journey.
  • Engagement. Earn video views, post interactions, messages, and follows, and build an audience you can retarget later.
  • Leads. Collect contact details through instant forms, Messenger, calls, or your own site, and route them straight into your sales process.
  • App promotion. Find people likely to install your app and take meaningful actions inside it.
  • Sales. Keep customers engaged and ready to purchase or complete another high-value action. Whether it is a purchase, a booking, or a request for quote, a sales-focused campaign is what sustains the relationship past the first click.

Ad quality

How can you tell good Meta ads from bad ones?

Not all Meta ads perform at the same rate. With the platform putting a growing premium on good customer experience, you need high-quality ads to drive conversions. Poor ones earn reduced distribution and flags that drag down the rest of your account.

01 / Relevance

Targeted and relevant

A good ad is built for a specific audience poised to interact with your brand and buy, not broadcast at everyone in the hope that someone bites. Relevance is what the auction actually rewards.

02 / Craft

Highly visual

Strong ads use the best-performing creative formats (photo, video, carousels, and more), with copy that is closely aligned to the image rather than fighting it for attention.

03 / Direction

A clear call to action

A button to click or a landing page to visit, with copy that earns the click and a destination that matches what the ad promised. Most weak ads fail here, not in the creative.

04 / Warning

Engagement bait

Spammy content that begs for likes, shares, and comments. It is one of the fastest ways to get an account’s distribution throttled, and the engagement it buys is worthless anyway.

05 / Warning

Withheld information

Purposely holding back detail to force a click. It inflates click-through rate and destroys conversion rate, which is the opposite of the trade you want.

06 / Warning

Sensationalized copy

Exaggerated headlines that do not match the information on the landing page. The mismatch is measurable: it shows up as a bounce, and the platform notices before you do.

07 / Outcome

Reduced distribution

Low-quality ads do not just underperform. They get flagged and throttled, and the damage carries across the rest of the account. Meta puts a growing premium on customer experience, which means ad quality is not a creative preference. It decides how much of your budget the platform is willing to spend on your behalf.

Practical decisions

Cost, transitions, and how to judge the work.

How much do Meta ads cost?

Meta advertising’s biggest strength is keeping costs low while holding one of the highest returns among paid channels. There is no fixed price: what you pay per click or per 1,000 impressions moves with your audience, objective, placement, and season. Budgets can start small and scale as results come in.

You stay in full control of the spend. Costs are driven by the audience you want to reach, your average bid, the campaign objective, placement, ad quality, season, and industry. Those levers are what allow small businesses to compete in a market like Toronto on modest budgets.

  • Bidding strategy. Ads are typically billed cost per click, cost per engagement, or cost per thousand impressions. Pick the model that matches your goal.
  • Campaign objective. Awareness, traffic, engagement, leads, app promotion, or sales. Broader audiences cost less; higher-value target actions cost more.
  • Audience size. Broad, early-funnel audiences are less competitive. Narrowing to precise characteristics raises the bid because you are competing harder for a smaller group.
  • Click-through rate. A high CTR is rewarded with lower costs over time. A low one signals a mismatch between your ad and your audience, and pushes acquisition costs up.
  • Budget and management. Set a realistic budget and account for the learning phase, which costs more while the platform works out who to show your ad to. Then weigh the management fee against the difference expert campaign management makes.

What happens to my current Meta ads service if I change providers?

Remember, they are your ads, your account, and your campaigns. You are in full control, and switching should be quick, simple, and safe.

  1. Inform your current agency, and check your business portfolio (formerly Business Manager). Confirm your business owns the ad account and that ads live inside it.
  2. Get a full snapshot of campaign performance so you have benchmark data to measure the new work against.
  3. Remove the old provider’s access to your account.
  4. Add your new agency as a Partner with the specific permissions they need to build and manage campaigns.
  5. Monitor the account to verify logins, maintain access control, and track performance from day one.

Handled this way, your account stays secure, your history stays intact, and you keep full visibility of what is running.

How to tell if your Meta ads agency is doing a good job

  • You are hitting your KPIs, whether that is clicks and engagement, landing page visits and form submissions, or sustained revenue.
  • The account history shows constant adjustment and optimization, including A/B testing of creative and audiences, not a campaign set live and left alone.
  • You receive regular, timely reports aligned to those KPIs, with clear recommendations for improving or launching campaigns.
  • Your dedicated account manager is responsive and communicates with you on a predictable schedule.

Who should own my Meta ad account?

Your business should own the ad account, the pixel, the audiences, and the creative, and retain administrator access. Your agency can be granted management permissions without taking ownership. A good agency insists on this arrangement rather than resisting it, because the alternative means your conversion history walks out the door when the relationship ends.
