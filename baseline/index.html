02 How it works

Your body already
makes it. You're just
running low.

Creatine's job is to hand a phosphate group to spent ATP so your cells can fire again. Your liver makes about a gram a day and red meat covers a little more, which leaves most people carrying stores well under capacity.

Five grams a day fills the tank in three to four weeks. Keep taking it and it stays full. Stop and it drains back to where you started, slowly, over about a month.

07 Questions

The ones worth
answering properly.

Do I need to do a loading phase?

No. Loading, usually around 20 grams a day split into four doses for five to seven days, gets your stores full in about a week instead of three to four. It's a speed option, not a requirement, and it's the main reason people report stomach upset. Five grams a day gets you to exactly the same place.

Will creatine make me hold water or look puffy?

Creatine draws water into the muscle cell, which is part of how it works, and that's different from holding water under the skin. Most people notice slightly fuller looking muscles rather than a soft or bloated look. If you load aggressively you're more likely to feel it, which is another argument for skipping the loading phase.

Is it safe long term?

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most examined supplements there is, with studies running for years in healthy adults and a strong safety record at normal doses. The kidney concern traces back to a misreading of creatinine markers rather than to actual harm in healthy people. If you have an existing kidney condition, are pregnant or nursing, or take prescription medication, talk to your doctor first. That's a genuine caveat, not boilerplate.

Do I need to cycle off it?

No. Your body doesn't stop producing its own creatine because you supplement, and there's no receptor to downregulate. Cycling just means your stores drop for a few weeks and you get less benefit during that window.

Does it matter what time I take it?

Far less than whether you take it at all. Saturation is a running total, so the goal is hitting five grams most days rather than timing it around a workout. Pick the moment you'll remember. For most people that's with breakfast or coffee.

Why monohydrate and not HCl or a buffered version?

Because monohydrate is the form nearly every study used, and the alternatives have never shown a meaningful advantage in head-to-head work. They're mostly a way to charge more for less creatine. We'd rather be boring and correct.

Is creatine only for people lifting weights?

It works best paired with resistance training, since the training is what drives the adaptation. That said, the research on older adults maintaining muscle and on cognition under fatigue reaches well past the gym. Plenty of our customers have never touched a barbell.

How long until I notice anything?

Two to four weeks at five grams a day, and it shows up as performance rather than a sensation. Creatine isn't a stimulant, so there's no hit. If you're tracking anything, track reps at a given weight.
